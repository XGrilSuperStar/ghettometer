Frequently asked questions

What does the score mean?

It runs from 0 to 100. Lower is nicer, higher is worse. The score compares a neighborhood to every other neighborhood in the dataset, so 70 means the area ranks worse than roughly 70 percent of them on the factors you have turned on.

What do the dot colors mean?

Green means a nicer area, yellow means mixed, and red means worse. Turn on "Score dots" in the Layers panel to see them. The color key at the bottom of the panel shows the full range. If you pick a single factor from the "Show" menu in the top bar, the dots switch to that factor's color and get brighter where it is stronger.

What are the colored dots for places?

Each place type has its own color: orange for fast food, pink for strip bars, green for dispensaries, yellow for cash loan places, purple for public housing, white for grocery stores, teal for parks and light blue for transit. Zoom in to see them. They are the actual locations behind the numbers.

Can I change what counts?

Yes. In the Layers panel, uncheck "Score" next to any factor to leave it out of the calculation. The score updates right away. "All score off" and "All score on" reset everything at once.

How accurate is it?

It is as accurate as the public data behind it. Census numbers are estimates, business locations can be out of date, and the score is only a summary. Treat it as a fast first look, then confirm by visiting. See How it works for a full list of limits.

Why is my neighborhood scored worse than I expected?

Scores are relative and count only the twelve listed factors. A leafy area next to a busy commercial strip can pick up fast food and cash loan counts. Check the breakdown under the dial to see which factors pulled the score. If something looks wrong, tell us.

Does it include crime?

No. Crime data is not consistent across the country, so it is not part of the current score. Check your local police department's published crime maps for that.

Does it cover the whole country?

The map covers the United States. Coverage and data completeness can vary by area, so rural places and recently built neighborhoods may have thinner data.

What is the 3D button?

It shows buildings in three dimensions around the spot you are viewing, plus the places in that area. It is useful for getting a feel for density and how close things are.

Is it free?

Yes. The site is supported by advertising.

Do you store my searches?

Gettometer does not require an account and does not ask for your name. See the Privacy Policy for exactly what is and is not collected.

Is this meant to label people or communities?

No. The name is tongue in cheek. The score describes measurable things around a location, such as stores, parks and income levels. It says nothing about the character of the people who live there.

Can I use the data or the map on my own site?

Please contact us first. The underlying sources are public, and each has its own license.
