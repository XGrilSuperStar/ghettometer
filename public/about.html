About Gettometer

A free map that shows what a neighborhood is really like, before you rent, buy, or move.

What it is

Gettometer is an interactive map of the United States. Search an address or city, or click anywhere on the map, and a dial shows a score from 0 (nice) to 100 (worst) for that spot. Under the dial you can see exactly what drove the score: how many fast food restaurants, cash loan shops, dispensaries and strip bars are nearby, and how the area compares on income, education, parks, grocery stores, public transit, school quality and home value growth.

Why it exists

Listings show you the kitchen and the square footage. They do not tell you what is within a mile of the front door. People deciding where to live, buy, invest or open a business usually end up driving around, reading scattered reviews, and checking half a dozen websites. Gettometer puts the public data in one place so you can see the picture in seconds.

Who it is for

  • Renters and home buyers comparing neighborhoods
  • Real estate investors screening areas
  • Families weighing school quality, parks and grocery access
  • Anyone relocating to a city they have never visited

Where the data comes from

Gettometer uses public and open data: U.S. Census Bureau American Community Survey for income and education, HUD for public housing, Zillow's published home value index for price trends, Overture Maps for business and place locations, and public school test score data. Base maps come from the USGS National Map. The 3D view uses OpenStreetMap data. The full list, and how the numbers are combined, is on the How it works page.

What it is not

A score is a summary of measurable facts, not a judgment of the people who live somewhere. It does not measure friendliness, community spirit, or how safe any individual street feels. Use it as one input alongside visiting in person. See the FAQ for more on its limits.

Built independently

Gettometer is built and maintained by an independent developer based in Southern California. It is free to use. Ads help cover hosting and data costs. Questions, corrections and data suggestions are welcome on the Contact page.
